# How we should present this to the client

## What to say before opening the demo

“We've prepared a visual demonstration so you can review the direction of the website before we complete the real system. This is sample branding, packaging and pricing for review. Your actual logo, colors, product information and photographs will replace these examples after approval.”

## 5-minute demonstration

1. **Homepage (60 seconds):** Show navigation, premium design, smooth animation, responsiveness and 3D jar rotation.
2. **Products (60 seconds):** Show the collection, filter/sort, product detail and add-to-bag feature.
3. **Cart and checkout (60 seconds):** Show quantities, delivery calculation and clearly state that it is not a real payment.
4. **Admin workspace (90 seconds):** Show an owner editing products, prices, and availability. Explain that secure admin sign-in and real order management come in phase two.
5. **Collect feedback (30 seconds):** Ask the client to confirm the design direction, required pages, product types, delivery policy and payment method.

## What we must never claim

- That the current dashboard is secure or actually authenticates administrators.
- That the checkout is connected to PayHere or accepts payments.
- That any sample orders or reviews are genuine.
- That the fictional food, nutrition, product or brand descriptions are verified.
- That placeholder policies constitute legal compliance.
- That animations, colors or templates have already been approved by the client.

## What to confirm at the design milestone

- Real brand assets and spelling
- Approved fonts and colors
- Pages and navigation
- Catalog categories / sizes / variations
- Photos or 3D packaging assets provided by client
- Cash on delivery and/or online payments
- Shipping areas, rate rules and taxes
- Must-have admin operations
- Applicable food disclosures and reviewed terms/policies

Only treat written approval as final design sign-off; major scope changes require a separate estimate.
